6  Fall 2026 Roadmap — experiments to December

Published

August 25, 2026

Working plan from today (Aug 25) to the results freeze in December. The top-level Gantt is the source of truth for dates; this page holds the reasoning behind them: research questions, week-level milestones, the quantifiables spec (metrics, generalization tiers, failure taxonomy), the literature map, the open-source stack, and how AI research assistants are used. Revise it whenever the plan changes — it becomes the skeleton of the Methods chapter and the defense timeline slide.

6.1 Research questions

The thesis claim is built around three questions. Every experiment should answer one of them; anything that doesn’t is scope creep.

RQ1 — Adaptation. Can current open vision-language-action (VLA) foundation models (NVIDIA GR00T N1.5, SmolVLA, optionally π0) be adapted to a new embodiment — the OARbot (Clearpath Dingo omni base + Kinova Jaco Gen2 6-DOF arm) — with a modest demonstration dataset collected in the lab?

RQ2 — Comparison & generalization. On a fixed task suite, how do the fine-tuned VLAs compare against each other and against a from-scratch imitation baseline (ACT) trained only on our demonstrations — both on in-distribution success rate and on how gracefully performance degrades across generalization tiers (unseen poses, distractors, novel object instances, paraphrased instructions)? The baseline is what makes generalization attributable: if the VLAs degrade less than ACT out of distribution, that gap is the measured value of internet-scale pretraining.

RQ3 — Practicality. What are the real compute and data costs — fine-tuning wall-clock on a DGX Spark, demos-per-task needed, on-robot inference rates — and what does that imply for small-lab replication?

RQ3 is the differentiator: most VLA papers report results on the authors’ hardware fleet; a careful account of what it takes to adapt these models on one robot with two lab machines is a genuine contribution.

6.2 Compute and platform allocation

Machine Role
DGX Spark (GB10, 128 GB unified memory) All fine-tuning: GR00T N1.5 (NVIDIA ships a DGX Spark fine-tuning playbook for it), SmolVLA full fine-tune, π0 LoRA if attempted
RTX workstation (“7080” — confirm exact model, likely RTX 5080) Policy inference at eval time (joins the robot’s ROS 2 graph over the network), dataset QC and visualization
OARbot’s own computers Teleop and demo recording happen here, not on the RTX box: Surface Go 2 tablet + SpaceMouse/deadman/E-stop is the operator interface; the helper laptop is the hub that drives the Kinova arm (USB-B) and runs the device nodes; the Dingo onboard PC runs the ROS 2 Jazzy base; the NUC handles overhead-camera pose estimation (per the OARbot docs)

Keep training and robot operation on separate machines from day one so data collection never competes with training runs.

6.3 Month-by-month plan

6.3.1 September — bring-up, task suite, literature (epics B1, A2, A1)

  • Week of Sep 1: Full teleop chain on one OARbot: drive the base, command the arm (SpaceMouse), stream both Azure Kinects. Verify E-stop and deadman behavior. Note which sensors are actually usable (docs already flag the UWB units as inoperable).
  • Week of Sep 8: Record demonstrations into LeRobotDataset format — joint states + base velocity + camera frames, replay a recorded episode open-loop on the robot to prove the pipeline is lossless. This replay test is the single most important de-risking step of the semester.
  • Week of Sep 15: Freeze the task suite (epic A2): 3 tasks in increasing difficulty, e.g. (T1) tabletop pick-and-place, arm only; (T2) fetch — navigate to a table, grasp, return; (T3) a true mobile-manipulation task with base+arm coordination (open a door, clear a table). Scope grows in that order by design: start arm-only with the base parked, expand only once T1 works. Even so, freeze the full 10-D action/state schema (6 arm + 3 base + gripper) from the first recorded episode — base dims logged as zeros while parked — so widening scope later means new data, never a pipeline rewrite. Define per-task success criteria, the staged progress rubric (reach / grasp / transport / place), the in-distribution randomization grid, and the generalization tiers G1–G4 (see the quantifiables spec below) before collecting data. Confirm the suite with the advisor. Also start the action-space decision log: every embodiment-design choice (joint deltas vs. absolutes, base twist representation, chunk length, control rates, normalization) gets a dated entry with the reasoning — this log becomes the Methods section on adapting VLA action heads to a mobile manipulator.
  • Week of Sep 22: Pilot data collection: ~10 episodes per task, train a quick SmolVLA sanity run on T1, fix whatever breaks.
  • Gate 1 (Sep 26): teleop + record + replay all work, task suite signed off. Fallback if late: drop T3 base motion and run an arm-centric thesis — decide here, not in November.

6.3.1.1 Data-pipeline decisions (locked 2026-08-25)

  • Teleop is Cartesian (SpaceMouse → EE twist → Kinova IK); training actions are joint positions derived from achieved states: action(t) = joint angles at t+Δ. Teleop space and training space are decoupled by this labeling — the raw SpaceMouse commands are never used as labels.
  • Recording = ros2 bag record on the helper laptop (compressed image topics, one auto-numbered bag per episode), then an offline bag → LeRobotDataset converter: uniform rate, nearest-frame alignment, the frozen 10-D schema, t+Δ action labels, instruction string per episode. Raw bags are kept forever as source of truth — every labeling choice stays reversible without re-collection.
  • Wrist camera mounted before any kept demos — observations are locked in at recording time; actions can be re-derived, images cannot.

6.3.1.2 Week-1 verification checklist (at the robot)

  1. ros2 topic list + ros2 topic hz during teleop: arm JointState topic (finger joints included?), camera topics, /odom, raw teleop command topic if exposed. Note exact names and rates.
  2. Angular control — does the lab stack expose joint position/velocity streaming to the Gen2? Teleop only proves the Cartesian path; policies need the joint path. Biggest unknown on the platform.
  3. Gripper commands land on a ROS topic and appear in recordings.
  4. Round-trip latency: camera → RTX box → command back to the helper laptop (decides wireless inference viability; chunk ≈ 3× latency).
  5. Clock sync (chrony) across Dingo PC / helper laptop / NUC / RTX box — required for correct chunk alignment at deployment.
  6. End-to-end proof: record one episode → convert → replay it on the arm via angular control.

6.3.2 October — dataset at scale + first baseline (epics B2, C2)

  • Weeks of Oct 5 / Oct 12: Main collection push, target 50–100 episodes per task (ACT/SmolVLA-class results are routinely reported around 50 demos/task; more helps T3). Interleave tasks and vary object poses per the randomization grid. Nightly QC script: episode counts, joint-limit outliers, dropped frames.
  • Week of Oct 19: SmolVLA fine-tuned per-task on the Spark, and ACT trained from scratch on the identical data — it trains in hours and rides along on the same LeRobot dataset, giving the no-pretraining reference point for RQ2. First closed-loop rollouts on the robot.
  • Week of Oct 26: Start GR00T N1.5 fine-tuning (epic C1) — the new-embodiment head needs the most debugging lead time. First structured mini-eval (10 trials/task) of SmolVLA.
  • Gate 2 (Oct 31): SmolVLA achieves non-zero success on T1 with a documented eval protocol. If zero: freeze collection, debug the data/normalization/camera pipeline first — more bad data won’t help.

6.3.3 November — GR00T + full evaluation (epics C1, C3)

  • Weeks of Nov 2 / Nov 9: GR00T N1.5 tuned across tasks; top-up data collection where QC shows gaps; π0 (LoRA via LeRobot/openpi) only if both main models are stable.
  • Week of Nov 16: Full evaluation begins (epic C3): ≥20 trials/task/policy on the shared in-distribution grid, everything in the quantifiables spec logged per trial, video of every trial. Budget realistically — 3 tasks × 4 policies (ACT, SmolVLA, GR00T, π0-if-attempted) × 20 trials at ~3 min/trial is ~12 robot-hours per full round, plus resets.
  • Gate 3 (Nov 27): Decide final comparison set (ACT + 2 or 3 VLAs) and confirm the data-scale ablation (retrain at 25/50/100 demos on one task) fits the calendar.

6.3.4 December — finish, freeze, outline (epics C3, D)

  • Weeks of Dec 1 / Dec 7: Complete in-distribution eval rounds + data-scale ablation; generalization round — tiers G1–G4 on each model’s best checkpoint only (10 trials/tier, see quantifiables spec).
  • Week of Dec 14: Re-runs and holes only. Results freeze Dec 18 — after this date, no new experiments, only analysis.
  • Week of Dec 21: All results tables and figures generated by script from logged data; chapter outline drafted in chapters/.

6.3.5 January–March 2027

Chapter drafts Jan 4 – Feb 26 (Background and Methods first — most of Methods should fall out of this page and the epic READMEs), full draft to advisor end of February, defense mid-March (confirm the exact date and committee logistics with the advisor in September, not January).

6.4 Quantifiables — what gets measured and reported

What the results chapter must contain, modeled on what this class of papers (ACT, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA, π0, SmolVLA, GR00T, Mobile ALOHA) actually reports. Frozen in epic A2 before data collection; every eval trial logs a row with all of these fields plus a video path.

6.4.1 Decision thresholds — what “achieved” means

Targets are pre-registered thresholds, not promises. A miss, analyzed honestly, is still a result.

  • Platform validated: ≥1 policy reaches ≥70% success on T1 in-distribution.
  • Pretraining transfer confirmed: the VLAs beat from-scratch ACT on the generalization tiers by ≥15 percentage points average. A null or negative gap = “pretraining did not pay off at this data scale on this embodiment” — a publishable negative result.
  • Practicality documented: complete cost sheet (below) for every model, no gaps.

6.4.2 Primary metrics (every policy, every task)

Metric Definition Reported as
Success rate Binary per trial against the frozen rubric; human intervention = failure % with 95% Wilson CI, n ≥ 20
Progress score Staged credit: reach 0.25 / grasp 0.5 / transport 0.75 / place 1.0 (per-task rubric) Mean 0–1; separates “never grasps” from “drops at the end” when success rates are low
Time to completion Successful trials only, start signal → success condition Median [IQR] seconds

6.4.3 Secondary metrics

Metric Definition Reported as
Inference latency Camera frame in → action chunk out, on the RTX workstation ms per chunk + achieved control rate (Hz)
Generalization gap In-distribution success minus per-tier success Percentage points, per tier per policy
Data efficiency Success rate at 25 / 50 / 100 training demos (one task, all policies) Curve — the small-lab money figure

6.4.4 Generalization tiers (RQ2)

Evaluated on each model’s best checkpoint only, 10 trials/tier, selection rules frozen in A2:

  • G0 — in-distribution: objects and poses from the training grid.
  • G1 — spatial: object poses outside the training grid.
  • G2 — visual: distractor objects + lighting change.
  • G3 — object: novel instance of the same category (different mug, different box).
  • G4 — language: paraphrased instruction, same task (“pick up the cup” → “grab the mug”). VLA-only tier — ACT has no language input, which is itself part of the comparison story.

6.4.5 Failure taxonomy

Every failed trial gets a first-failure label from this closed list, logged at eval time (10 seconds of effort) with the video path:

perception/grounding (approached wrong object) · navigation/base-pose (stopped out of reach) · grasp (contact but slip/miss) · manipulation-trajectory (jerky/unsafe motion, joint limit) · instruction (did the wrong task) · stall/timeout · hardware (excluded and retried, not counted).

Rendered as stacked bars per policy × task — this figure explains why the success rates differ and is the backbone of the analysis chapter.

6.4.6 Practicality cost sheet (RQ3)

Per model: fine-tuning wall-clock hours and peak memory on the DGX Spark, trainable parameter count (LoRA vs. full), GPU-hours per training run, on-robot inference hardware requirements. Plus data cost: minutes per collected episode, total collection hours, retake rate.

6.4.7 Deliverables checklist (results chapter)

  • T1 Main results: success ± CI, task × policy, in-distribution
  • T2 Generalization matrix: policy × tier
  • T3 Efficiency: latency, control rate, completion time
  • T4 Cost sheet (RQ3)
  • F1 Data-scale curve · F2 Failure-mode stacked bars · F3 Qualitative rollout film-strips (best + worst per policy)

Protocol hygiene: the randomization grid and rubrics are frozen before collection; checkpoint selection criteria defined before eval; all runs reported (no cherry-picked seeds); every trial has video.

6.5 Literature map

Organized by the Background chapter’s likely sections. Maintain citations in Zotero and export to references.bib (keep BibTeX keys stable). Verify every reference on arXiv/Semantic Scholar before it enters the bib file — never cite from an LLM’s memory.

1. Learning from demonstration — foundations

  • Argall, Chernova, Veloso, Browning (2009), A survey of robot learning from demonstration — the canonical survey to anchor the chapter.
  • Pomerleau (1988), ALVINN — behavioral cloning origin.
  • Ross, Gordon, Bagnell (2011), DAgger — compounding-error argument every IL thesis needs.

2. Modern imitation learning architectures

  • Zhao et al. (RSS 2023), ACT / ALOHA — action chunking; the direct ancestor of today’s VLA action heads.
  • Chi et al. (RSS 2023), Diffusion Policy — generative action heads.
  • Lipman et al. (ICLR 2023), Flow Matching — theory behind π0-style and GR00T-style flow action heads.
  • Fu, Zhao, Finn (2024), Mobile ALOHA — the closest published setup to this thesis: whole-body IL on a mobile manipulator.

3. Vision-language-action models

  • Brohan et al., RT-1 (2022) and RT-2 (2023) — the VLA lineage.
  • Open X-Embodiment collaboration (2023/24) — cross-embodiment pretraining; why adapting a generalist to OARbot is plausible at all.
  • Octo Model Team (2024) — open generalist policy.
  • Kim et al. (2024), OpenVLA — open 7B VLA + fine-tuning recipes (see also the OpenVLA-OFT follow-up on efficient fine-tuning).
  • Black et al. (2024), π0; Physical Intelligence (2025), π0.5 — flow matching VLA, open weights via openpi.
  • Hugging Face (2025), SmolVLA — small (~450M) VLA designed for exactly this compute class; likely the workhorse baseline.
  • NVIDIA (2025), GR00T N1 paper (+ N1.5 release notes) — the primary model; its dual-system architecture and new-embodiment fine-tuning story are directly load-bearing.
  • Google DeepMind (2025), Gemini Robotics — closed, but frames the state of the art in Background.

4. Mobile manipulation and assistive context

  • TidyBot (2023), HomeRobot/OVMM (2023), BEHAVIOR-1K (2023) — mobile-manipulation task framing and benchmarks.
  • RPI’s own OARbot publications — get the list from the advisor; the assistive-robotics motivation should cite the lab’s line of work.

5. Datasets and evaluation methodology

  • DROID (2024), BridgeData V2 (2023), AgiBot World (2025) — large-scale teleop dataset practice worth imitating at small scale.
  • LIBERO (2023) — task-suite design and eval discipline.
  • SimplerEnv — Li et al. (2024), Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation — the real-vs-sim eval gap.
  • Recent work on statistically sound robot policy evaluation (trial counts, confidence intervals, A/B protocols) — good target for a deep-research sweep, see below.

6.7 Using AI research assistants

Division of labor — each tool where it is strongest:

  • Gemini Deep Research — breadth: one report per Background section above (“annotated bibliography of VLA fine-tuning on novel embodiments 2023–2026, with arXiv links”). Also the eval-methodology sweep in §5.
  • ChatGPT (deep research) — cross-check Gemini’s reports; protocol questions (“how many trials for a detectable 15-point success-rate difference between two policies?”).
  • NotebookLM — load the collected PDFs per chapter section; ask grounded cross-paper questions while writing Background.
  • Claude Code — everything in-repo: LeRobot/GR00T pipeline code, QC scripts, eval analysis, figures, Quarto chapter drafting, and keeping this PM surface honest.

Standing rule: LLM output is a lead, never a citation. Every claim that survives into the thesis gets verified against the primary source, and every reference is checked on arXiv before entering references.bib.

6.8 Risk register

Risk Likelihood Mitigation
Bring-up eats October (drivers, arm, cameras) High Gate 1 fallback: arm-only task suite, decided Sep 26
Demo data too little/noisy for T3 Medium Tiered tasks — T1/T2 alone still answer RQ1–RQ3; data-scale ablation turns “not enough data” into a finding
GR00T new-embodiment head underperforms Medium SmolVLA is the guaranteed baseline; a negative GR00T result, carefully analyzed, is still a thesis result
Eval time underestimated High Protocol fixed in Sept; ≥20 trials budgeted on the calendar, not squeezed in
Generalization round balloons eval time Medium Tiers only on best checkpoints, 10 trials/tier; if squeezed, run G1–G4 on T1+T2 only
Two-robot temptation (multi-robot experiments) Medium One OARbot is the experiment platform; the second is spare parts / backup until the freeze
Writing starts late Medium Section D outline bar in December; Methods accretes from epic READMEs all semester
